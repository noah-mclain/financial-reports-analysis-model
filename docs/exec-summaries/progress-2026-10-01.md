## Risks to the date

1. **Scanned accuracy is the critical path.** 85.83% against 98%, and four balance sheets not extracted at all. The OCR bake-off decides whether scanned Arabic can meet the gate or has to be shown as held for review. Everything downstream (mapping, metrics, narration) is only as good as this on scanned filings.
2. **The GGUF spike is late and guards week 3** (R16). If the fused adapter does not convert, the Docker profile needs another route for the model, and that is better known before training starts than after.
3. **`model_test` has never been run.** Gates A and B need it, and the first run freezes the `model_test` and `blind` pools. When to take that checkpoint is still an open decision.
4. **Nothing exists yet beyond extraction**: no analytics package, no model code, no API or page. This is as scheduled, but it means three of the four weeks' deliverables are still ahead with 25 days left.
5. **Depth on extraction costs days.** Today's branch review ran six passes. It found real faults, but that depth applied to every later part will not fit.

## What I would do with week 2

1. **Tomorrow, before week 2 starts:** the GGUF spike, time-boxed to half a day. It is the cheapest way to remove the largest unknown.
2. **Docker skeleton,** minimal: one image, compose, a health page. Half a day.
3. **OCR bake-off,** time-boxed to two days, on the scanned golden pages with the extraction eval as the score. Cut RapidOCR if time runs short and compare Vision with Tesseract only.
4. **Label mapping,** aliases and structural anchors first; fuzzy matching only if the critical items are not all mapped.
5. **Analytics:** identity checks and the metrics the summary will cite, with the caveat stage. Trim towards 8 metrics before trimming correctness.
6. **Bank and insurer decline, then the first blind run,** which needs the `model_test` checkpoint decision.

Per the plan's rule, an overrun is cut inside its own week. The cuts above are the ones that cost least.
