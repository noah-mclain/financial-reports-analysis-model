Agreement with MLX by quantization level (quantization stores the weights in fewer bits to shrink the file). The route was run twice, the second time with `llama-server`'s prompt cache switched off:

| Model | File | Bits per weight | Same answer as MLX, second run | First run |
| --- | ---: | ---: | :---: | :---: |
| GGUF f16 | 994 MB | 16.00 | 10 / 10 | 10 / 10 |
| GGUF Q8_0 | 531 MB | 8.50 | 9 / 10 | 9 / 10 |
| GGUF Q6_K | 506 MB | 8.09 | 10 / 10 | 10 / 10 |
| GGUF Q5_K_M | 420 MB | 6.71 | 9 / 10 | 9 / 10 |
| GGUF Q4_K_M | 398 MB | 6.35 | 7 / 10 | 8 / 10 |

The plan's bar was 9 of 10. The file named Q4_K_M misses it. Task 1's done-when (one route gives the same id as MLX on at least 9 of 10) is met at f16, Q8_0, Q6_K and Q5_K_M; at Q4_K_M, the level the plan's step 5 names, it is not. Three things limit what the table says:

- **These files are not the levels their names say.** The 0.5B model's rows are 896 wide, and the K formats need a multiple of 256, so `llama-quantize` fell back to finer formats on 144 of 290 tensors. The "Q4_K_M" file is 6.35 bits per weight, where a true Q4_K_M is about 4.9. The table says nothing about a true Q4_K_M on the 7B. The route's fuse and convert steps carry over from the small model; the quantize step does not.
- **Which answers change.** Q8_0 changes only "revenue from contracts with customers". Q5_K_M changes only "profit for the period". Q4_K_M changes both, and in the second run also "investment income". Q6_K changes none.
- **The prompt cache changes an answer on its own.** The same Q4_K_M file answers "investment income" one way with the cache on and another with it off, each repeatably. Switching the cache reproduces the difference on the same file; the first run's GGUF files were not hashed, so that the two runs' files were otherwise identical is not shown.

In the f16 model the three prompts that change are the three with the narrowest margin between first and second choice: log-odds of 0.008, 0.018 and 0.289, where 0 is a tie. That is consistent with near ties flipping under any rounding. It is not shown: the margins were read from the GGUF and not from MLX, the third is not a tie (its first choice is 1.3 times as likely as its second), and nine of the ten margins are under 1.4. Ten prompts cannot tell a tie from a real loss. The level has to be chosen on the real adapter.

"Same answer" is the measure, not "right answer". The toy adapter names the right id on only 2 of the 10 unseen labels.

## What this adds to weeks 2 and 3

- **OpenCV does not import in the image.** `import cv2` fails with `libxcb.so.1: cannot open shared object file`. The lock has `opencv-python`, not the headless build, and the slim base image lacks five system libraries. The OCR bake-off needs either those libraries or the headless wheel in the lock, and the image needs the Linux OCR engines anyway.
- **Smaller items the image will meet in weeks 2 to 4:** the `app` user cannot write under `/app/var` until a volume is mounted for artifacts and the docling model cache; services have no memory limits; `llama.cpp:server` and `python:3.12-slim` are floating tags to pin by digest when the model service first runs; and there is no restart policy until the job queue arrives.

Carried into week 3, before and around the training run:

- **Benchmark an off-the-shelf Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct Q4_K_M GGUF in the container before training.** Speed does not depend on the adapter, and R19's mitigation, a smaller model, needs its own training run.
- **Rehearse the 7B route with a throwaway adapter,** recording peak memory, disk and any quantize fallbacks.
- **The quantization level is chosen by measurement on the real adapter:** Q4_K_M against Q5_K_M on at least 50 held-out labels, taken from a validation split of `train` or `dev` issuers, never from `model_test` or `blind`.
- **The acceptance rule is fixed in advance:** a margin threshold in log-odds on both runtimes, and task accuracy against gold, not only agreement with MLX. For narration, the grounding checker's pass rate per profile, since exact match means nothing for prose.
- **Tokenizers:** compare `llama-server`'s `/tokenize` with the Hugging Face tokenizer on Arabic labels. Send `"cache_prompt": false` in every comparison.

## Decisions waiting

One is new. The three in the 1 October note are unchanged.

- **Amend the `--dequantize` rule for the GGUF export, or not.** `01-constraints.md` 1.4 forbids running `mlx_lm.fuse --dequantize` and downloading full-precision weights on this machine. The route runs the first, and creates full-precision weights locally without downloading them. At 7B the cost is an estimate: about 15 GB for the dequantized directory, about 15 GB for the f16 GGUF and about 5 GB for the quantized file, 35 GB while all three exist. `df` showed about 68 GB free when measured on 2 October (it moves by a few GB as caches grow), so about 33 GB would remain, above the 15 GB floor; together with the 25 GB that 1.4 budgets for the bake-off it would be about 8 GB, under it. The peak memory of dequantizing and converting the 7B on 18 GB is not measured. Until this is decided the route is proven but not permitted for the 7B. If the answer is no, there is no tested route: route B was not run, and as planned it starts from the full-precision base model, which the same rule forbids downloading.

## What was not done or not measured

- No 7B model was converted or run. The 7B intermediates are about 15 GB each (an estimate from 7.6 billion 16-bit weights), the memory and time to dequantize and convert it are not measured, and the speed is the open R19 number above.
- The spike cannot have shown, for the 7B: safetensors split over several shards; true K-quant levels; long prompts and long generations.
- Nothing in the spike ran in Docker or on Linux. The CPU figures come from `-ngl 0` on macOS.
- Agreement was measured on English labels only. Arabic is untested, including whether the two runtimes tokenize it alike.
- The margins were read from the f16 GGUF only, not from MLX.
- Route B (the base model as GGUF with the adapter loaded separately) was not run. Route A agrees at f16. At Q4_K_M it scored 8 of 10 and then 7 of 10, which under the plan would have triggered route B. Whether B would do better is not measured; it remains the fallback if the real adapter loses answers at the level that ships.
- The image was built on aarch64 only. x86-64 is not built.
- No OCR engine is in the image, and no model.
- Each task's diff was reviewed separately from the person who wrote it, and the whole branch once more before pushing. Those reviews changed two things: the image now installs from the lockfile, and the spike's file sizes and quantization claims were corrected.

As a consequence, the checkpoint is taken once, after all planned work is complete and every check passes, not in weeks 2 or 3; until then Gates A and B are reported on `dev` and on dry runs and marked "not yet measured on `model_test`". The week 2 "first blind run" was left open here and was decided later the same day (D17).
- **D14, scanned Arabic that misses the gate.** Shown in the demo as held for review, with its reasons; not cut, and not presented as correct ([08-revised-plan.md](../blueprint/08-revised-plan.md), R15). How such a filing is identified in the demo is not built yet.

- **D16, what Gate C is measured on before the checkpoint.** Delegated by the owner, who asked for "the most optimal method of measurement for it until then".
